## What this does and does not show

- The result is not evidence of three independently trained JEPA models. All
  runs use the same actor checkpoint, JEPA checkpoint, Reliability Ledger,
  protocol, and canonical scene manifest.
- The three replays have identical episode outcomes. The non-negative delta is
  therefore a deterministic replay result, not a cross-model generalization
  result.
- The active-search mechanism was not exercised: cautious reacquisition attempt
  steps were zero in every M3 run. The observed gain cannot be attributed to
  active search.
- One M0 success became an M3 timeout in every replay. Safety was preserved,
  but capability regressed on that episode. This is a release-blocking behavior
  for a complete closed-loop claim.

## Failure and routing signal

The degraded episode has 250 M3 `safe_hold` steps and no reacquisition attempt.
The M3 route candidates were geometrically valid and independently CBF-probed,
so the immediate diagnostic hypothesis is Ledger/ranker routing or a route
termination contract, not a physical safety violation. This agrees with the
settled route-12 attribution already recorded in
[`JEPA_SAFE_CAPTURE_V5_ROUTE12_SETTLED_THREE_SEED_REPORT_20260906.md`](JEPA_SAFE_CAPTURE_V5_ROUTE12_SETTLED_THREE_SEED_REPORT_20260906.md):
the selected route was not reliably the settled-best route, and removing
abstention alone did not produce a control gain.

The next debugging unit is therefore the single degraded episode, with an
offline comparison of:

1. recorded M3 route and Ledger state;
2. eligible CBF-verified routes that were rejected;
3. settled-best route and its first-step action;
4. the nominal M0 action and the first step at which M3 diverges.

No CBF margin, stale/OOD gate, `controlled_abort`, or raw-action guard is to be
relaxed during this diagnosis.
